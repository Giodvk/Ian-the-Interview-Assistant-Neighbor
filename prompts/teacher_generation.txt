You are an expert dataset annotator generating high-quality training data for an AI interview coaching system.

Your task is to transform one job interview question into a realistic interview interaction composed of:

1. a candidate answer;
2. constructive feedback on that answer;
3. one context-aware follow-up question.

The generated data will be used to fine-tune a language model. Therefore, realism, consistency, diversity, and strict adherence to the requested output format are essential.

INPUT

Role: {role}
Sector: {sector}
Interview stage: {interview_stage}
Interviewer type: {interviewer}
Question category: {question_category}
Question level: {question_level}
Candidate quality: {candidate_quality}

Interview question:
{question}

TASK

## 1. Generate the candidate answer

Generate a realistic answer as if it were spoken by an actual candidate during a job interview.

The answer must:

* directly address the interview question;
* be coherent with the specified role and sector;
* be appropriate for the interview stage and question level;
* sound natural and human rather than like a textbook or model answer;
* contain enough information to support meaningful evaluation;
* avoid unnecessary verbosity;
* avoid mentioning AI, datasets, prompts, scoring labels, or the requested quality level;
* vary naturally in structure, vocabulary, reasoning style, and level of detail across examples.

Adapt the answer strictly according to `Candidate quality`.

### If Candidate quality is `weak`

Generate an answer that would reasonably be considered below expectations for the specified role and question level.

The answer should still be plausible and relevant, but it must contain at least one substantial limitation, such as:

* superficial understanding;
* vague or unsupported reasoning;
* failure to address an important part of the question;
* oversimplification;
* missing justification;
* weak or missing example;
* technically incomplete reasoning;
* unclear explanation of decisions or outcomes.

Do not make the answer absurd, completely incorrect, or unrelated.

A weak answer must be clearly distinguishable from an average answer.

### If Candidate quality is `average`

Generate an answer that correctly addresses the main question and demonstrates reasonable understanding.

The answer should contain useful and relevant information, but also one or two meaningful limitations, such as:

* insufficient depth;
* incomplete justification;
* missing trade-offs;
* weak supporting evidence;
* limited specificity;
* an underdeveloped example;
* incomplete discussion of implications.

An average answer should be acceptable but not especially convincing.

It must be clearly stronger than a weak answer and clearly weaker than a strong answer.

### If Candidate quality is `strong`

Generate an answer that would reasonably be considered convincing for the specified role and question level.

The answer should:

* fully address the core requirements of the question;
* demonstrate strong understanding;
* provide clear reasoning;
* justify important decisions;
* include relevant details, examples, trade-offs, or consequences when appropriate;
* be coherent and professionally convincing.

Do NOT deliberately insert a weakness only to make the follow-up question easier.

A strong answer may still leave room for deeper exploration, but it should not contain an artificial omission or obvious flaw.

The follow-up question may extend the discussion by exploring:

* deeper reasoning;
* trade-offs;
* alternative approaches;
* changed constraints;
* consequences;
* edge cases;
* assumptions;
* scalability;
* evidence;
* decision criteria.

## 2. Generate feedback

Evaluate only the candidate answer you generated.

The feedback must:

* be concise but specific;
* reflect the actual quality of the answer;
* identify strengths when present;
* identify concrete weaknesses, omissions, vague claims, missing evidence, or opportunities for improvement when present;
* explain how the candidate could improve the answer;
* remain grounded in the original interview question and candidate answer;
* consider the role, sector, interview stage, question category, and question level;
* avoid introducing unrelated requirements;
* avoid rewriting the entire answer;
* avoid generic statements unless followed by a precise explanation.

For weak answers:

* clearly identify the main deficiency or missing element;
* explain what would be required to reach an acceptable answer.

For average answers:

* acknowledge the core correctness of the answer;
* identify the most important limitation preventing it from being strong.

For strong answers:

* primarily acknowledge what makes the answer effective;
* suggest refinement only when genuinely useful;
* do not invent weaknesses that are not actually present.

Vary the wording and structure of the feedback across examples.

Do not systematically use recurring formulas such as:

* "The candidate acknowledges..."
* "However, the answer lacks..."
* "The candidate could improve by..."
* "The answer is good but..."
* "The response demonstrates..."

Feedback may begin with:

* the strongest aspect;
* the main weakness;
* the most important recommendation;
* a direct evaluation of the reasoning;
* a specific observation about the example or decision.

Avoid repetitive sentence structures across records.

## 3. Generate one follow-up question

Generate exactly one follow-up interview question.

The follow-up must:

* depend directly on the content of the candidate answer;
* refer to a specific claim, omission, decision, example, assumption, trade-off, or implication from the answer;
* remain relevant to the original interview question and role;
* be appropriate for the interview stage and question level;
* not simply repeat or paraphrase the original question;
* not be generic;
* sound like a realistic question an interviewer could naturally ask next;
* contain only one main question.

Adapt the purpose of the follow-up to the candidate quality.

For a weak answer:

* clarify or probe a central missing, vague, unsupported, or incorrect point.

For an average answer:

* deepen an underdeveloped but relevant part of the answer;
* ask for stronger justification, evidence, specificity, or trade-off analysis.

For a strong answer:

* deepen the discussion rather than searching artificially for a flaw;
* challenge an assumption;
* explore an alternative;
* introduce a new constraint;
* probe a trade-off;
* ask about consequences, scalability, edge cases, or decision criteria.

Vary the form of follow-up questions naturally.

Possible forms include:

* clarification;
* justification;
* example or evidence;
* trade-off analysis;
* changed constraint;
* challenge to an assumption;
* alternative approach;
* consequence or risk;
* comparison between options;
* deeper technical or behavioral reasoning.

Do not repeatedly default to formulations such as:

* "Can you explain how..."
* "Can you give an example..."
* "Could you elaborate..."
* "How would you..."

Use natural variation in phrasing while keeping the question realistic.

OUTPUT REQUIREMENTS

Return only one valid JSON object.

Use exactly these keys:

{{
"candidate_answer": "...",
"feedback": "...",
"follow_up_question": "..."
}}

Do not include:

* markdown;
* code fences;
* explanations outside the JSON;
* additional keys;
* comments;
* headings before or after the JSON.

Ensure the JSON is syntactically valid and can be parsed directly by a standard JSON parser.
